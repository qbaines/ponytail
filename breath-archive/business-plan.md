# Breath Archive: lean business and editorial plan

## Concept

Publish a source-led history of breathing practices and healing traditions, using digitized historical texts as the starting point. Begin as one site with interlinked topic channels—not several thin sites competing for the same readers. Possible channels include South Asian yoga texts, ancient Mediterranean medicine, and East Asian movement and breath traditions, added only when reliable primary sources and knowledgeable reviewers are available.

The value is careful interpretation: identify the text and edition, explain historical context, distinguish traditional claims from modern evidence, and link readers to the source. The project is educational, not a health service.

## Audience and positioning

- Readers interested in history of medicine, yoga history, primary sources, and responsible wellness writing.
- Positioning: readable essays grounded in identifiable editions, with transparent limits and citations.
- Avoid promises of cures, longevity, or effortless income. Historical claims are not clinical proof.

## Initial publication

1. Publish a source guide for each tradition with the archive record, edition/translation details, and rights notes.
2. Build a small set of original essays that answer one specific historical question each.
3. Interlink related essays and maintain a references page; add email signup only after there is a sustainable publishing cadence.
4. Expand to another topic channel only after source quality, reader interest, and editorial capacity are demonstrated.

## Revenue experiments

Treat revenue as uncertain and delayed; there is no guaranteed passive income. Prioritize trust and useful content before monetization.

- **Display advertising:** consider only after traffic is meaningful, and keep ads clearly separate from editorial recommendations.
- **Affiliate links:** use only for relevant books or archival resources; disclose commissions beside the links and do not recommend products as treatments.
- **Optional reader support:** memberships or donations for source transcription and research notes, without paywalling safety corrections.
- **Sponsorships:** accept only transparent, topic-relevant sponsors; never let a sponsor approve medical claims or conclusions.

Do not sell supplements, medical devices, or treatment programs based on historical health claims. Do not use fabricated testimonials, scraped content, keyword-spun articles, or undisclosed affiliate links.

## Lean technical approach

- Start with static HTML and CSS: fast, accessible, inexpensive to host, and no application database or tracking scripts required.
- Organize each post as a standalone page; maintain a central index, source list, clear navigation, and standard metadata.
- Deploy through a static host and configure a custom domain only when the owner supplies one.
- Add analytics, newsletter tools, structured data, and automation only when there is a concrete need and privacy implications are understood.
- Keep the site independent of the Ponytail package and publish it from this directory or move it into its own repository before a production launch.

## Editorial standards

- Cite exact work, translator/editor, publication date, and stable archive or scholarly record.
- Distinguish a work's traditional/estimated date from a surviving edition, translation, scan, and upload date.
- Use original summaries; do not republish scanned pages or substantial copyrighted translations. Verify the rights statement for each edition and jurisdiction.
- Attribute medical or spiritual effects to the historical source as claims, not facts.
- When discussing present-day health research, link to peer-reviewed reviews or primary studies, describe limitations, and avoid implying that evidence for one protocol applies to all practices.
- No individualized diagnosis, treatment advice, or instructions for forceful breathing or prolonged breath retention. Correct errors promptly and show update dates.
- Seek review from a qualified historian or tradition-informed scholar for claims that could misrepresent a living tradition; seek clinical review for any health-evidence claims.

## Measures of progress

Track whether readers use the work, not just page views: citations clicked, return visits, newsletter opt-ins, corrections received, and time spent maintaining each article. Review revenue and costs quarterly; pause channels that cannot be supported with accurate research.

## Current starter sources

- Svātmārāma, *The Hatha Yoga Pradipika*, translated by Pancham Sinh (1914), [Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/hathayogapradipi0000panc).
- Hippocratic corpus, “On Breaths,” in *The Genuine Works of Hippocrates*, vol. II, translated by Francis Adams (1849), [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/genuineworksofhi02hippuoft/page/186/mode/2up).
- “Exploring the Therapeutic Benefits of Pranayama (Yogic Breathing): A Systematic Review,” *International Journal of Yoga* (2020), [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336946/).

These are starting points, not a substitute for checking the relevant pages, scholarly context, and the rights status of the specific edition before publication or reuse.
